"It Doesn't Have to Be Crazy at Work" is a rejection of the chaos worshipped by hustle culture in the modern workplace. 
Jason Fried and David Heinemeier Hansson (DHH), founders of Basecamp, argue that a calm, focused work environment leads to better productivity and employee satisfaction. 
They don’t just question hustle culture—they put it on blast. 
Meetings? Mostly useless. 
Deadlines? Rarely real. 
MS Teams? A digital anxiety machine. 
They walk through the practices Basecamp lives by: what’s stuck, what’s been scrapped, and how they’ve been able to build a "calm" company.

## Curb Your Ambition
The authors argue that ambition in the workplace is often poorly directed and misplaced.
They don't say that goals can be a double edged sword, they discuss how Basecamp doesn't use any goals.
Their company runs on a unique schedule, instead of using the traditional fiscal year or quarter, they use 6 week development cycles with breaks of 2 weeks to reflect, reset, and prepare for the next 6 week cycle.

An area they don't discuss in detail, but I think is relevant here is the word "impact".
Many of the reasons that big companies operate they do is because of this word.
I think this concept significantly inhibits the ability of companies to move forward and truly make an impact.
At a big company, where dozens of people have the same exact role, operating in different spaces, it feels important to need to make an impact and differentiate.
